# Current G response: light vignette and gentle wear

This evidence supersedes the earlier v1.0.8 blackout-duration experiment. Current behavior has no blackout, redout, physiological exposure or control lock. Signed G and flight envelopes remain honest.

`measure.mjs` generates `measurements.json`: 120 seconds at loads from −40 to +40 G at 30/60/144 Hz never causes blackout/redout or disables controls. Vignette stays at or below 0.12. Ordinary loading produces no wear; prolonged excessive loading causes at most ten HP of repairable wear and cannot kill the aircraft.

Tests in `tests/pilot-load.test.js` cover signed physics, flight-path envelopes, unrestricted inputs, legacy snapshot sanitizing, bounded vignette, delayed gradual nonlethal wear, and existing home repair. At +9 G, four seconds causes no damage; 15 seconds costs only1.5HP. Wear does not alter engine/control health.

`tests/g-response-browser.cjs` uses one explicit airborne normal-mode fixture at3000m, then native V/A keys and ordinary animation frames. It holds A for15simulation seconds and releases forfour. No postfixture state writes or manual stepping. Browser assertions require a light vignette, transparent center, no blackout/redout, slight wear, no hit flash and full visual recovery. This is a turn/visual test, not a runway takeoff test. Screenshots and `browser-report.json` are generated by that test and visually inspected after the run.

Final browser run passed. At about +8.1 G after the held turn, vignette was about 0.092 and wear about 1.4 HP; there was no blackout, red tint or hit flash. After release, loading returned to +1.0 G and vignette to zero. The final held-turn and recovered screenshots were opened and inspected: clear center/instruments throughout, with a small right-side systems note during wear, removed after unloading. Zero page errors.
