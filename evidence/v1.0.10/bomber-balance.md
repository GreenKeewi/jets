# Bomber acquisition and altitude

The original B-2-inspired aircraft gets a fictional, deliberately modest low-observable advantage. Aircraft missile acquisition range is 2.8 km against bombers, versus 4.2 km against fighters. Acquisition builds at half the normal rate: approximately 0.9 seconds for a heat launch and 2.9 seconds for radar, versus 0.45/1.45 seconds. These are game balance values, not claims about real signatures or sensor performance. The existing aiming cone, target identity checks, lock reset on switching/out-of-range, countermeasures and missile damage remain. Bombers can still be acquired and shot down. Cinematic airfield SAM rules are unchanged.

AI strike bombers now command approximately 2,900 m cruise/escape altitude. The outbound altitude schedule descends over the last 11.5–4.5 km toward the bombing approach; real terrain clearance still takes priority. Position is never snapped to the commanded altitude. Runway departure, physical climb performance and bomb-release geometry remain in effect. Player bombers receive no automatic altitude target or forced dive at cruise height; the existing 6,000 m world ceiling is unchanged.

Regressions cover acquisition range and timing at 30/60/144 Hz, successful missile launch, lock identity changes, player climb above AI cruise, and actual AI takeoff/bomb/escape paths from every supported airfield at 30/60 Hz. Actual sampled sorties reached 2,874 m (attack) and 2,881 m (defend); bomb releases were at 1,079 m and 868 m respectively. Those deterministic routes completed in 172.65 and 207 seconds without crashes. This is simulation verification, not a claim of real B-2 capabilities.
